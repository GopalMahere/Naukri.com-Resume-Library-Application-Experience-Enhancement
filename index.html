Slide 1 — Welcome & Proposal Introduction

Good morning. My name is Aditya Sharma. Today I am sharing an independent Product Management investigation and proposal for Naukri.com titled 'Apply with the Right Resume'.

Job seekers frequently apply across varied roles—such as Product Management, Consulting, Analytics, or Engineering. Tailoring resumes to these distinct opportunities is common practice. However, when it comes to submitting the right document during the job application process, managing and switching between multiple resumes can introduce friction.

This proposal explores a candidate-controlled Resume Library for Naukri.com, enabling candidates to maintain role-specific resumes in one place and choose the appropriate document when applying.

Slide 9 — Current-State Journey & Branching Paths

A major flaw in naive case studies is assuming that a platform has only one rigid workflow. In reality, Naukri supports different application types:

  • Path A: In application flows that offer an alternate file upload, the candidate can upload locally. However, this file is not necessarily saved into an organized, reusable library.
  • Path B: In flows that rely on the profile-attached resume, the candidate may feel compelled to leave the application flow, update their profile resume, and re-navigate back. This introduces drop-off friction.
  • Path C: External employer redirects send candidates to third-party ATS platforms where resume handling is outside Naukri's control.
  • Recognizing these three paths makes our product proposal realistic and scoped specifically to supported on-platform journeys.

What May Be Causing the Friction?

Diagnostic problem tree separating observed symptoms from root-cause hypotheses

Observed & Reported Symptoms Symptoms
Surface Friction Experienced by Candidates
📁 Fragmented Storage: Candidates store files across laptops, Google Drive, and phones.
🔄 Repeated Overwrites: Updating profile resume replaces the previous file.
🚪 Flow Disruption: Navigating away from application to update profile file.
❓ Active CV Ambiguity: Uncertainty over which document recruiters currently see.
⏳ Postponed Applications: Candidates deferring applying when resume isn't ready.
Diagnostic Hypotheses to Investigate 5 Hypotheses
Underlying Structural Hypotheses
H1. Separation of Document vs. Application: Resume management is architecturally decoupled from the moment-of-apply decision point.
H2. Absence of Persistent Role Library: In the flow studied, candidates lack a single home for named, reusable role-specific documents.
H3. Journey Option Variance: Different application types expose varied upload capabilities, leading to inconsistent mental models.
H4. Destructive Overwriting: Profile settings treat resume upload as file replacement rather than version addition.
H5. Traceability Gap: Application history may not visibly reflect which exact resume version was attached for past submissions.
Rigor Note: These are hypotheses formulated to guide investigation. We do not claim insight into Naukri's proprietary internal codebase or service architecture.

Slide 11 — Root-Cause Analysis

In product design, treating surface symptoms without understanding root causes leads to ineffective features:

  • Symptoms: Candidates juggle files across devices, overwrite previously uploaded resumes, or navigate away during active applications.
  • Underlying Hypotheses:
    1. Resume storage is decoupled from the application decision.
    2. There is no dedicated, persistent library of role-specific documents within the candidate profile.
    3. Different job types have different application flows, causing candidate confusion.
    4. Uploading a new document in profile settings often replaces the existing file destructively.
    5. Application history may not preserve an immutable reference to the submitted document.
  • By addressing these root causes, our proposal aims to resolve the underlying friction.

Slide 13 — Strategic Alternatives Evaluation

Before committing to a product design, a strong PM examines multiple alternatives:

  • Option A (Status Quo): Low engineering cost, but leaves candidate friction unresolved.
  • Option B (Multiple Profiles): Maintaining separate profiles fragments search history, causes duplicate candidate records, and creates high maintenance overhead.
  • Option C (One-Off Uploads): Uploading every time offers flexibility, but provides zero reusability, forcing candidates to repeatedly re-upload files on their phones or laptops.
  • Option D (Resume Library - Recommended): Offers the best combination of candidate control, persistent organization, and non-destructive saving within a single profile.
  • Option E (AI Auto-Matching): Premature for MVP. Candidates want full agency over what recruiters receive; automated selection introduces mistrust and error risk.

Slide 14 — The Three Core Solution Pillars

The proposed Resume Library consists of three complementary pillars:

  • Pillar 1: One Comprehensive Profile: We maintain a single structured master record for candidate credentials. We do not make premature claims about internal recruiter search algorithms, but keep structured profile data conceptually separate from resume files.
  • Pillar 2: Dedicated Resume Library: A persistent document vault where resumes are stored as independent entries with custom names. Editing or deleting one file never affects others.
  • Pillar 3: Application-Time Selection: Job seekers can preselect a default resume for convenience, choose an alternate saved document, or upload a fresh file.
  • Core Principles: Privacy by default (recruiters see only the attached document), zero silent overwriting, and immutable application history tracking.

Slide 18 — Experimentation & Metrics Framework

To establish whether the feature improves the candidate experience, we propose a controlled A/B experiment:

  • Eligible Cohorts: Cohort A experiences the baseline application flow; Cohort B receives the Resume Library and application-time selector.
  • Primary Metric: Application Completion Rate (Completed Applications / Application Starts). This measures whether candidates are more likely to finish applying when resume-switching friction is mitigated.
  • Secondary Metrics: We track adoption—how many candidates store multiple resumes, the ratio of existing selection vs. fresh uploads, and save adoption.
  • Guardrail Metrics: Accidental overwrite incidents must strictly remain zero. We also monitor wrong-resume support tickets and upload failures.
  • Statistical Parameters: We do not invent arbitrary conversion numbers or guaranteed lifts. The analytics team must determine sample size, MDE, and duration based on Naukri's actual volume.

Appendix C: Product Terms, Assumptions & Technical Glossary

Product Glossary & Assumptions Terminology
Resume Library: A candidate-controlled collection of versioned resume documents with custom user-defined names, stored within the candidate's account.
Master Profile Record: Structured candidate career information (education, work history, skills) that remains the central record for recruiter search.
